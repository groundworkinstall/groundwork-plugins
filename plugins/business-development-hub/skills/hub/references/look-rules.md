# Rules for anything people look at

1. A picture earns its place. If a sentence says it better, use the sentence.
2. One idea per page, slide or post.
3. Pick the right form: a table to compare, a short list for steps, a figure for a trend, a sentence for one point.
4. Headings say what the section does or what it shows, not a label.
5. Charts share one scale. No pie chart with more than three slices.
6. Titles say the point ("Sign-ups doubled in March"), not the topic ("Sign-ups").
7. Label things directly, not with a legend off to the side.
8. Arrows mean "leads to" or "comes next", nothing else.
9. One style through the whole piece: same fonts, same colors, same spacing.
10. Light background, dark type. No dark panels behind body text.
11. Readable at real size: on a phone for posts, across a table for slides, at arm's length for print.

## Defaults when no brand is set

White or off-white background, near-black text, one accent color used once per view, soft gray for lines and notes. No orange, red or bright yellow as the accent unless it's the brand.

## Shapes by piece

- **One-pager:** a title that says the point, three to five short sections, contact line at the bottom.
- **Slides:** one point per slide, the title is the point, eight slides or fewer for a meeting.
- **Social post:** one idea, a few words on the image, the logo small in a corner.
- **Proposal:** what they asked for, what you'll do, when, what it costs, what happens next.
- **Web page:** what you do and for whom in the first screen, then proof, then how to reach you.

## The check, before anything ships

1. Does it say one thing?
2. Does every title say the point?
3. Are the colors the brand's (or the working look's), with the accent used once per view?
4. Light ground, dark type?
5. One font family for headings and one for text?
6. Readable at real size?
7. Any placeholder, bracket or "lorem" left? (There can't be.)
8. Names, dates and numbers right?
9. Does every picture earn its place?
10. Is the logo the owner's file, unstretched?
11. Does the writing follow the Writing style section?
