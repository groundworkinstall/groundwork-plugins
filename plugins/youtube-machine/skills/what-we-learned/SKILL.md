# What we learned

Read the last four finished videos' briefs, interview notes and packages, the What we know doc, any viewer or client questions saved in the Topics tab or the video Notes, and the last field scan. Get the time from the app's time tool before you write any date.

## The numbers, read by a person

You can't open YouTube Studio, so the keeper reads the numbers to you. If the fourth video is under 7 days old, offer to wait a week first, because the numbers are thin before then. Default: wait.

For each of the four videos, give these steps (screens from YouTube's help pages; YouTube is rolling out a new Studio look from July 2026, so send a screenshot if one doesn't match):
1. Go to studio.youtube.com. Click Content on the left, then the video, then Analytics.
2. Click Reach. Read out Impressions, Impressions click-through rate, Views, and the top three traffic sources. If it shows YouTube search terms, read the top five.
3. Click Engagement. Read out Average view duration, the video's length, and any key moments it shows (intro, top moments, spikes, dips).
4. "Not enough data" or a dash is an answer. Say that and go on.

Save the numbers in a Google Doc `Numbers: videos [first #] to [last #]` in the machine's folder, one short table per video.

Two things to say once, so nobody reads too much into it: YouTube counts a view the moment a video starts playing (since Aug 2026), so view counts read higher than they used to. And numbers on new, small videos swing a lot, so compare their own videos with each other, never with other channels.

## Turning the numbers into one change

Pick the first one that fits, and only one:
- Most videos under 100 views: too early to read. Take the change from the questions below instead.
- Few impressions and little search traffic: write the next titles from the search words that did bring people.
- Fine impressions, but the lowest click-through each time: plainer thumbnail words.
- Good click-through, but short viewing: fix the first 30 seconds (the outline's opening).
- A low intro or the same dip in several videos: change the opening, or shorten that kind of part.
- The email list didn't grow: say the checklist earlier in the video, and check the channel's advanced features are on (the Settings tab's `Advanced features`).

Write the change on the Settings tab as `Change for next four`, in one line. The research-brief and interview skills read it for the next four videos.

## Then, in one message

1. **The one change,** in one line, and the numbers that led to it.
2. **Three things you noticed,** one line each: a question that keeps coming up, a point the speaker makes every time, a gap nobody else is filling.
3. **Up to four options to make next,** each with one line on what it is, who it's for and how long it'd take them:
   - a new free checklist (it grows the email list),
   - a talk they can offer to employers, groups or events,
   - a workshop or class,
   - a new shortcut skill in Claude for a step they keep doing by hand (say which step).
4. **Your pick and a default**, so one word settles it.

When they pick, draft it as a Google Doc in the machine's folder titled `[What it is]: [topic]`, in their voice from the interviews, for them to change and approve. A skill idea is written as plain steps in the doc; it becomes a real skill only when they ask.

Add a `Learned` Log row naming the change, what was picked and the doc's link. That row resets the four-video count.
